## Conceito

A capa concentra a narrativa em uma mecânica de Super Feka Gaps: Joãozão golpeia o chão e abre um buraco. Na ilustração promocional, esse evento é ampliado para uma fenda dramática. Feka escapa, o minion reage, e o capacete funciona como recompensa visível na outra margem. A ilustração não é uma captura literal da fase.

A direção busca a linguagem de impressão e desenho das embalagens do fim dos anos 70 e início dos 80. O título conserva SUPER amarelo, FEKA vermelho e GAPS azul. As faixas mantêm os textos solicitados, Remaster e new graphics.

## Decisões de composição

| Elemento | Função na capa |
| --- | --- |
| Fenda diagonal | Organiza o espaço e liga o nome GAPS ao perigo que o jogador enfrenta. |
| Golpe de Joãozão | Mostra a causa do perigo, em vez de apenas posicionar o inimigo no fundo. |
| Salto de Feka | Dá ação à cena, mantendo óculos, cabelo escuro, pele morena e roupa azul reconhecíveis. |
| Minion na borda | Acrescenta humor e uma reação ao mesmo acontecimento. |
| Capacete e cristais | Trazem objetos e materiais do jogo como detalhes secundários. |
| Letras geométricas | Dão uma assinatura ao título; o recorte do A em GAPS ecoa a fenda. |
| Azul profundo e pedra clara | Separam o vazio das superfícies e deixam as cores dos personagens aparecerem. |
| Moldura e faixas | Compartilham alinhamentos e margens; Remaster tem maior peso que new graphics. |

## Referência consultada

A [retrospectiva oficial de Pitfall!, da Activision](https://blog.activision.com/activision/2019-04/RetroVision-Feature-Part-1-Happy-37th-Birthday-Pitfall-Harry) apresenta a embalagem original de 1982. Ela foi observada como referência de hierarquia, composição com áreas de cor e relação entre a mecânica do jogo e a ilustração. A composição de Super Feka Gaps é nova, sem reutilizar a arte ou as marcas da referência.

A pesquisa orienta o briefing; a imagem final é uma interpretação contemporânea, sem alegação de ser uma capa histórica.

## Prompt final

```text
Create one finished, exceptionally art-directed VERTICAL 2:3 game box front for SUPER FEKA GAPS. This is a NEW composition with a new visual concept, not a variation of a smiling cartoon boy jumping among coins. The target is the expressive printed cartridge packaging and adventure illustration of 1978–1983: an image with a memorable IDEA, beautifully drawn characters, original lettering and exacting graphic design.

THE SINGLE IDEA: THE FLOOR IS THE TRAP.
The game's green villain Joãozão punches the stone floor and opens a gap under Feka. Show the most suspenseful, playful instant of that event. A dramatic dark zigzag fissure runs diagonally through the image, from the lower foreground toward the villain at the far upper-right. This abyss is the principal graphic shape and the visual signature of the cover. Its angular silhouette connects the action, the word GAPS and the clipped geometry of the frame. Every object participates in this story. No generic platform-game collage.

SCENE / COMPOSITION
A tilted three-quarter view of an ancient blue-stone arena, its floor breaking into three broad, beautifully composed planes. The gap takes up meaningful negative space. Five or six angular fragments are suspended near the breaking edge; controlled impact lines describe the villain's strike. There is depth and clear cause and effect.
Feka dominates the middle-left, dynamically clearing the fissure with a natural athletic leap, one hand open for balance, legs bent in DIFFERENT directions, torso turning back to watch the villain, a small courageous wry smile. His whole silhouette reads instantly. He is neither posing at the viewer nor throwing a punch. Moderate foreshortening, normal-size shoes. He occupies about one third of the image area, not the whole image.
Joãozão is smaller at the far upper-right of the arena, one huge fist planted on the cracked floor, shoulders leaning into the impact, a cunning delighted grin: clearly HE is causing the gap. He is stocky and square, not a superhero bodybuilder.
One little red minion peers nervously over a nearer broken edge, adding humour through its relationship to the abyss rather than being pasted into a corner.
A GOLD construction helmet rests on the intact far platform, a small reward and an actual game power-up. A few sharp turquoise crystals are visible deep within the gap. Two indigo stone arches at the rear establish the fortress without turning into a fantasy-castle panorama. The setting is graphic and economical: no floating coin trail, no smiling hills, no waterfalls, no generic grassy floating islands, no sunburst behind the hero.
Light warm stone planes face deep ultramarine/cyan shadows. The image promises skill, danger and playful adventure, never horror.

CHARACTERS — DRAW THEM AS DISTINCTIVE CLASSIC ADVENTURE ILLUSTRATION
Feka is a Brazilian-looking young ADULT with warm medium-brown skin, short neat black hair with a modest square silhouette, expressive thick RECTANGULAR BLACK GLASSES, a plain short-sleeve COBALT BLUE shirt, dark blue trousers and dark practical shoes. Give him character in his facial expression and hands; stylised believable adult proportions, approximately six heads tall, a normal head, no gigantic eyes, no extravagant anime quiff, no chibi body, no babyface. No cap, moustache, gloves, cape or weapon.
Joãozão is a broad blocky GREEN ogre with short dark-green hair, heavy brows, a squared face and mischievous teeth; PURPLE sleeveless outfit and trousers, small gold belt buckle. Keep his squat weight and theatrical expression; avoid muscular-superhero anatomy.
The minion is a simple squat RED creature with large white eyes, black brows and tiny dark feet, no beak.

ILLUSTRATION / PRINT LANGUAGE
Authentic late-1970s / early-1980s commercial illustration: expert pen-and-ink drawing, varied line weight, precise confident contour, selective fine hatching used ONLY to articulate important shadow planes, beautifully simplified anatomy and architecture. Broad opaque SOLID spot-colour shapes; warm ivory, saturated vermilion, golden yellow, cobalt blue, turquoise, olive-lime green, plum and deep midnight ink. Vivid colour with disciplined distribution. Just two tones per material.
The result should have the craftsmanship of an original hand-drawn arcade campaign and the graphic clarity of a screen-printed adventure poster. Not modern vector clip art, not a children's-book cartoon, not mobile-game advertising, not 3D CGI, not a contemporary fantasy digital painting. No noisy fake ageing, no distressed filter, no dirt, no brushy texture, no smooth glossy gradients. Crisp clean printing, tiny selective line detail, strong masses of colour.

PACKAGE AND ORIGINAL LOGOTYPE
Full-bleed midnight-navy cover field. An elegant thin ivory rule frames the cover with generous breathing room; the rule is interrupted intentionally by the corner bands. The art fills the lower roughly 72% inside this organised system. The artwork and masthead belong to one designed object.
At the top-left, compact bespoke 1979-style geometric display lettering:
"SUPER" in solid golden YELLOW, smaller.
"FEKA" in solid vermilion RED, large.
"GAPS" in solid vivid cobalt/sky BLUE, large.
FEKA and GAPS share a coherent tight wordmark, straight baselines, slightly extended geometric letters with purposeful angular inner cuts. Let a small angular cut in the lettering subtly echo the abyss. All words completely readable. Warm-ivory narrow keyline only as needed for contrast. Flat graphic lettering, NO puffy cartoon type, no glossy bevel, no inflated 3D extrusion, no generic comic font, no giant white sticker outline. The title occupies approximately 20% of the height, safely clear of the picture.

THE EDITION BANDS ARE PART OF THE DESIGN
At the TOP-RIGHT CORNER only, a refined pair of slim parallel diagonal printed bands at 45 degrees, designed together with the frame. Reserve this corner in advance. Neither band cuts through the logo or a character.
Primary band: warm ivory with navy condensed lettering reading exactly "Remaster".
Secondary narrower band: golden yellow with small navy lettering reading exactly "new graphics".
The two bands use the same carefully spaced condensed typographic family, calibrated margins and a narrow navy separation. They run from the top edge to the right edge. The primary label has greater emphasis than the secondary. No oversized opposing corner sashes, no wooden signs, no plaques, no seals, no starbursts, no folded cloth or fake shadows. Both texts must be fully legible and uncut. Keep the labels subordinate to the illustration.

Only these exact five text strings: "SUPER", "FEKA", "GAPS", "Remaster", "new graphics".
No publisher names, console logos, model numbers, dates, ratings, fake awards, invented taglines, or watermarks. One flat high-resolution portrait front-cover image, not a photographed box. Final visual priorities: recognisable original characters; unforgettable diagonal abyss; authentic period drawing; exact restrained package geometry; flawless lettering.
```
